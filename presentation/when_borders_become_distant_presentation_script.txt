WHEN BORDERS BECOME DISTANT 20-minute presentation speaking script

General delivery note

Aim to finish the prepared presentation in about 18.5-19 minutes rather
than filling every second of the 20-minute slot. Slow down on the
treatment construction, main results, and event-study slides. The
central story is not simply that a coefficient is negative; it is that
GIS gives a much better measure of exposure, while the econometric
diagnostics limit the strength of the causal interpretation.

Do not apologize for the pre-trend results. Present them as an important
result of testing the research design.

============================================================ SLIDE 1 -
TITLE Target: ~1 minute
============================================================

Good afternoon, and thank you for being here.

This paper asks a simple geographical question with an economic
consequence: what happens when an international border remains
physically close, but becomes much more difficult to reach through an
operational crossing?

The key idea throughout the presentation is that border access is not
the same thing as straight-line distance to a boundary. What matters for
economic interaction is whether a municipality can reach a crossing that
is actually operational, and how far it must travel through the road
network to do so.

I study this question along the Colombia-Venezuela border, focusing on
the disruption of formal border connectivity beginning in February 2019.

The title, “When Borders Become Distant,” refers precisely to this idea:
the border does not move geographically, but policy can make it
effectively much farther away.

============================================================ SLIDE 2 -
MOTIVATION Target: ~2 minutes
============================================================

The Colombia-Venezuela border is an especially useful setting because
large parts of the frontier have historically functioned as integrated
economic and social spaces.

Cross-border connections matter for trade, employment, transport,
services, household relationships, and commercial activity. This is
particularly important around the Norte de Santander-Tachira corridor.

A closure therefore does not affect every municipality in the same way.
Imagine two municipalities that are both relatively close to the
international boundary. One may still have direct access to an
operational bridge. The other may suddenly need to travel several
hundred additional kilometres to reach the next usable formal crossing.

That gives us the first motivation for the paper: proximity to the
border is not sufficient to measure exposure to a border-access shock.

The second motivation is measurement. Comparable high-frequency local
economic statistics are difficult to obtain across both countries, and
this is especially problematic for Venezuela during its economic crisis.

Nighttime lights are useful in this setting because they provide the
same independently collected measure on both sides of the border.

So the project combines two types of spatial information: road-network
accessibility tells us where the shock was stronger, while VIIRS
nighttime lights provide a common proxy for how local economic activity
evolved.

============================================================ SLIDE 3 -
INTRODUCTION / RESEARCH QUESTION Target: ~1.5 minutes
============================================================

The central research question is:

How were changes in road-network access to the Colombia-Venezuela border
related to local economic activity?

The paper makes three main empirical contributions.

First, instead of assigning municipalities to a simple treated and
untreated group, I construct a continuous treatment measure based on the
change in road-network distance to the nearest operational international
crossing.

Second, I combine this measure with a municipality-quarter panel of
VIIRS nighttime lights.

Third, I evaluate the relationship under increasingly demanding spatial
controls. I begin with a conventional two-way fixed-effects model and
progressively absorb country-, crossing-corridor-, and state- or
department-specific quarterly shocks.

An important part of the project is that the interpretation evolved as
these diagnostics became more demanding. The initial estimate looks
consistent with the expected negative effect, but the localized
specifications and event study show that exposure was also related to
broader local economic dynamics.

So the paper is not only about estimating a border-closure coefficient.
It also evaluates how far this spatial design can support causal
interpretation in this particular setting.

============================================================ SLIDE 4 -
CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK Target: ~2 minutes
============================================================

The mechanism has three pieces.

First, a policy decision changes which formal international crossings
are operational.

Second, this changes effective transportation geography. Municipalities
previously connected to a nearby crossing may have to take substantially
longer routes.

Third, if cross-border accessibility matters economically,
municipalities experiencing larger accessibility losses should
experience weaker local economic activity.

This framework is closely related to the spatial-accessibility approach
developed by Behrens in 2024.

An important clarification is that this project is not a replication of
Behrens. I implement the central spatial-accessibility logic in a
different institutional and geographic setting: the Colombia-Venezuela
frontier.

The project therefore sits at the intersection of three literatures:
borders and trade costs, transportation and market access, and remotely
sensed measures of economic activity.

The distinctive element here is their combination. A policy-driven
change in the border regime is translated into a municipality-specific
change in effective road accessibility and then linked to
satellite-observed local outcomes.

The expected relationship is straightforward: larger deteriorations in
effective accessibility should be associated with weaker economic
activity.

============================================================ SLIDE 5 -
METHODOLOGY: EMPIRICAL PIPELINE Target: ~2.5 minutes
============================================================

The empirical construction proceeds in five steps.

First, I build a motorcar road network for Colombia and Venezuela using
OpenStreetMap.

Second, I define the relevant crossing regimes by identifying which
formal crossings are operational before and after the 2019 disruption.

Third, I calculate shortest-path road-network distances from
municipalities to the operational crossing system.

Treatment intensity is defined as:

D_i = log(d_i post) - log(d_i pre).

This is easier to understand with an example.

Suppose a municipality was 50 kilometres by road from its nearest
operational crossing before the disruption. After that crossing becomes
unavailable, its nearest operational alternative is 200 kilometres away.

Its treatment intensity is therefore log 200 minus log 50, which is log
4, or approximately 1.39.

Now consider another municipality whose distance changes only from 100
to 120 kilometres. Its treatment intensity is only log 1.2, or about
0.18.

So both municipalities experience the same broad political event, but
the first is much more strongly exposed because the closure causes a
much larger proportional deterioration in accessibility.

A municipality whose nearest operational crossing does not change would
receive a treatment intensity of zero.

Importantly, D_i is municipality-specific; it is not recalculated as a
different treatment value every quarter in the baseline model.

In the fourth step, I interact D_i with the post-period indicator.
Before the disruption, Post equals zero, so the interaction is zero.
During the clean closure period, Post equals one, so the municipality’s
full treatment intensity enters the regression.

Finally, I estimate fixed-effects models and dynamic event-study
specifications.

============================================================ SLIDE 6 -
METHODOLOGY: BASELINE MODEL / IDENTIFICATION Target: ~2 minutes
============================================================

The dependent variable is quarterly municipality-level log one plus
nighttime lights.

Municipality fixed effects absorb time-invariant local characteristics,
while the time controls absorb common or increasingly localized shocks.

The coefficient of interest is the interaction between treatment
intensity and the post period.

Timing is important here.

Although VIIRS is available for a much longer period, I do not simply
use every available observation.

I begin the baseline estimation in 2017 because 2015 and 2016 contained
an earlier major border closure and a subsequent partial reopening.
Including those years as if they represented the same stable pre-2019
regime would contaminate the pre-treatment comparison.

So 2017Q1 through 2018Q4 is the clean pre-period.

The disruption occurred in February 2019, so I exclude 2019Q1 as a
transition quarter rather than classifying the entire quarter as treated
or untreated.

The clean closure period then runs from 2019Q2 through 2022Q2.

I stop there because formal connectivity begins changing again in
September 2022. Once crossings begin reopening, the original closure
treatment no longer represents the actual accessibility regime. Those
later observations are therefore used separately for the reopening
exercise.

I then estimate a specification ladder: conventional TWFE,
country-by-quarter effects, crossing-corridor-by-quarter effects, and
finally state- or department-by-quarter effects.

The purpose is to ask whether the relationship survives when
progressively more localized time-varying shocks are absorbed.

============================================================ SLIDE 7 -
DATA Target: ~1.5 minutes
============================================================

The empirical analysis combines four main spatial data components.

Administrative boundaries define the municipality-level panel.

OpenStreetMap provides the motorcar road network used to calculate
shortest-path accessibility.

Formal international crossings are georeferenced and combined with
institutional information on their operational status.

Finally, quarterly VIIRS nighttime lights provide a geographically
consistent proxy for local economic activity on both sides of the
border.

The estimation window shown here is deliberately narrower than the raw
satellite-data coverage. It is determined by the institutional regimes
rather than by satellite availability.

As I mentioned, 2017Q1 to 2018Q4 forms the clean pre-period, 2019Q1 is
excluded as a transition quarter, and 2019Q2 to 2022Q2 forms the clean
closure period.

The initial geographic analysis contains more municipalities, but the
baseline estimation sample is restricted to municipalities that can be
reliably connected to the road network under the required accessibility
regimes.

The final baseline sample contains 192 municipalities and 4,032
municipality-quarter observations.

============================================================ SLIDE 8 -
RESULTS: MAIN ESTIMATES Target: ~2 minutes
============================================================

This figure summarizes what becomes the central empirical result.

In the conventional two-way fixed-effects specification, the coefficient
is negative, approximately minus 0.017, and statistically significant at
conventional levels.

So initially, municipalities experiencing larger increases in effective
distance appear to experience weaker nighttime-light outcomes after the
disruption.

But as the fixed effects become more demanding, the coefficient
attenuates.

With country-by-quarter effects, it falls to approximately minus 0.010
and is no longer statistically significant.

With crossing-corridor-by-quarter effects, it becomes smaller again.

And with state- or department-by-quarter fixed effects, the estimate is
approximately minus 0.006 and statistically indistinguishable from zero.

This attenuation is substantively important.

It suggests that part of the negative TWFE relationship was associated
with spatially heterogeneous economic dynamics that were themselves
correlated with treatment exposure.

For that reason, I do not interpret the first coefficient as the clean
causal effect of the border disruption.

The more defensible conclusion is that there is an initial negative
relationship, but its magnitude and precision are highly sensitive to
how localized time-varying shocks are controlled for.

============================================================ SLIDE 9 -
RESULTS: DYNAMIC EVIDENCE / PRE-TRENDS Target: ~2 minutes
============================================================

The event study is what makes that caution especially important.

The omitted period is 2018Q4, and the coefficients show how the
relationship between eventual treatment intensity and nighttime lights
evolves around the 2019 disruption.

If the parallel-trends assumption held cleanly, we would expect the
pre-treatment coefficients to fluctuate around zero without a systematic
relationship with subsequent exposure.

That is not fully what we observe.

Some pre-treatment coefficients are already different from zero.

Under the preferred state- or department-by-quarter specification, the
joint pre-treatment test gives an F-statistic of approximately 2.32 and
a p-value of 0.027.

This is weaker evidence of differential trends than in the simpler
specifications, but the null of no differential pre-trends is still
rejected at the five-percent level.

This result changed how I interpret the empirical design.

Rather than adding controls until the diagnostic happens to disappear, I
treat the failed pre-trend test as information: municipalities that
later received different levels of the accessibility shock were already
following different economic trajectories.

The event study therefore limits how strongly the average post-treatment
coefficient can be interpreted causally.

This is not a failure of the GIS treatment measure. It is a distinction
between measuring treatment accurately and identifying its causal
effect.

============================================================ SLIDE 10 -
RESULTS: REOPENING / COMPLEMENTARY EVIDENCE Target: ~1.5 minutes
============================================================

The later reopening provides a complementary exercise with the opposite
direction of the accessibility shock.

Beginning in September 2022, formal cargo connectivity was progressively
restored through important crossings.

The reopening estimates produce a pattern that is broadly consistent
with the proposed accessibility mechanism: municipalities more exposed
to the earlier deterioration in access show comparatively stronger
outcomes when formal accessibility begins to improve.

However, I interpret this cautiously.

The reopening event study also shows pre-period movement, and the
reopening coincided with other political and economic changes.

So I do not treat this as a second clean natural experiment.

Instead, I view it as complementary mechanism-consistent evidence: the
direction is informative, but it does not overcome the identification
concerns found in the main analysis.

The country-specific evidence likewise shows heterogeneity across the
two sides of the border, but the estimates become less precise under
more demanding local controls. I therefore avoid interpreting them as
clean opposite causal effects for Colombia and Venezuela.

============================================================ SLIDE 11 -
CONCLUSION Target: ~1.5-2 minutes
============================================================

I would summarize the project with three conclusions.

First, border policy substantially changed effective economic geography.

The road-network calculations show that changing the availability of a
small number of formal crossings generated very large accessibility
losses for some municipalities while barely affecting others.

Second, these accessibility changes are related to local economic
activity. The initial TWFE estimate is negative, and the reopening
pattern is consistent with the proposed accessibility mechanism when
access begins to recover.

But third, the econometric diagnostics prevent a strong causal
interpretation.

The main coefficient attenuates under increasingly localized fixed
effects, and differential pre-treatment dynamics remain.

For me, this leads to the broader methodological lesson of the project:

GIS can substantially improve the measurement of treatment exposure, but
precise spatial measurement does not automatically solve identification.

That distinction between treatment measurement and treatment
identification is probably the most important conclusion of the paper.

At the same time, in a setting where conventional local economic data
are difficult to obtain, combining road networks, institutional crossing
histories, and satellite observations makes spatial economic
relationships visible that would otherwise be difficult to study.

Thank you.

============================================================ Q&A
PREPARATION - SHORT ANSWERS
============================================================

1.  WHY START IN 2017 IF VIIRS EXISTS FROM 2012?

“VIIRS is available from 2012, but I begin the baseline estimation in
2017 because 2015 and 2016 contain an earlier major border closure and
partial reopening. Starting in 2017 gives me a cleaner and more stable
pre-treatment regime for studying the 2019 disruption. The estimation
window is therefore determined by the institutional design, not by
satellite-data availability.”

2.  WHY STOP AT 2022Q2?

“I stop the main closure analysis at 2022Q2 because formal connectivity
begins changing again in September 2022. Once crossings reopen, the
original closure treatment no longer describes actual accessibility. I
therefore treat 2022Q3 as a reopening transition and use the later
observations in a separate reopening exercise.”

3.  WHY EXCLUDE 2019Q1?

“The disruption occurred on 23 February 2019. Classifying the whole
quarter as either untreated or fully treated would be inaccurate, so I
exclude it as a transition quarter.”

4.  WHY USE LOG DISTANCE CHANGES?

“The log difference measures a proportional accessibility shock. For
example, 50 to 200 kilometres and 200 to 800 kilometres both represent a
fourfold increase and therefore receive the same treatment intensity.
This is preferable to treating a fixed kilometre increase as
economically identical regardless of the municipality’s initial
accessibility.”

5.  IS TREATMENT TIME-VARYING?

“In the baseline model, treatment intensity D_i is
municipality-specific. It is calculated from the change between the
pre-closure and closure crossing regimes. The time variation comes from
interacting that municipality-specific intensity with the post
indicator. The event-study version then interacts the same exposure
measure with relative-time indicators.”

6.  WHY NOT JUST USE DISTANCE TO THE BORDER?

“Because the policy shock operated through specific crossings, not
through the location of the international boundary. Two municipalities
equally close to the border can experience completely different changes
in effective access depending on the road network and which crossing
they use.”

7.  WHAT DOES GIS ADD?

“GIS reconstructs effective access conditional on the road network and
the operational crossing regime. It therefore allows the treatment to
reflect the actual transportation geography generated by the policy
change rather than a simple Euclidean border-distance measure.”

8.  WHY ONLY 192 MUNICIPALITIES?

“The baseline sample is restricted to municipalities that can be
reliably connected to the road network and assigned the required pre-
and post-regime accessibility measures. I prefer a consistent connected
sample rather than imputing unreliable network distances.”

9.  WHAT ABOUT INFORMAL CROSSINGS OR TROCHAS?

“The treatment measures formal road-based international connectivity. I
do not claim that all cross-border interaction stopped. Informal
crossings remained important, which is one reason the treatment should
be interpreted as a formal-accessibility shock rather than complete
border isolation.”

10. WHY NIGHTTIME LIGHTS?

“They provide a geographically consistent quarterly proxy observed in
the same way on both sides of the border. That is particularly useful
when comparable municipality-level economic statistics are difficult to
obtain, especially for Venezuela. I treat luminosity as a proxy for
local activity, not as literal municipality GDP.”

11. DOES THE SIGNIFICANT TWFE COEFFICIENT SHOW A CAUSAL EFFECT?

“No. The TWFE estimate establishes an initial negative relationship, but
it attenuates under more localized controls, and the event study rejects
clean parallel pre-trends. Those diagnostics are why the final
interpretation is deliberately more cautious.”

12. DOES THE FAILED PRE-TREND TEST MEAN THE PROJECT FAILED?

“No. It means the design does not support the strongest causal
interpretation. The treatment measure still captures substantial
heterogeneity in how border policy changed effective accessibility. The
diagnostic tells us that treatment exposure was also correlated with
pre-existing local economic trajectories, which is itself important for
interpreting the design.”

13. WHY USE STATE/DEPARTMENT-BY-QUARTER FIXED EFFECTS?

“They absorb shocks common to municipalities within the same state or
department in a given quarter. This is important because Colombia and
Venezuela, and even regions within each country, experienced very
different economic trajectories over the study period.”

14. WHY DOES THE COEFFICIENT SHRINK WITH MORE FIXED EFFECTS?

“That suggests part of the baseline negative association reflects
regional time-varying shocks correlated with treatment exposure. The
more localized models compare municipalities after absorbing those
common regional-quarter dynamics.”

15. WHAT SHOULD I SAY ABOUT THE REOPENING?

“The reopening provides complementary mechanism-consistent evidence, not
an independent clean causal estimate. The direction is consistent with
improved accessibility, but its own dynamic pattern and the broader
political changes around reopening require caution.”

16. WHAT ABOUT THE HIGH OVERALL R-SQUARED AND LOW WITHIN R-SQUARED?

“The high overall R-squared is largely driven by the fixed effects,
which absorb substantial persistent and time-varying spatial
heterogeneity. The within R-squared asks how much additional within-unit
variation is explained after those fixed effects are removed, so it is
naturally much smaller in a saturated fixed-effects model.”

17. WHY IMPLEMENT BEHRENS RATHER THAN CALL THIS A REPLICATION?

“The project takes the spatial-accessibility logic from Behrens - using
changes in effective access to international crossings - but applies it
to a different border, institutional history, unit of analysis, and
empirical setting. It is therefore an implementation or adaptation of
the approach rather than a replication of the original study.”

18. WHAT IS THE MAIN TAKEAWAY IF ASKED FOR ONE SENTENCE?

“Border policy substantially altered effective road-network
accessibility along the Colombia-Venezuela frontier, and those changes
are systematically related to local nighttime-light outcomes, but
pre-existing spatial trends prevent interpreting that relationship as a
clean causal effect.”

============================================================ DELIVERY
REMINDERS ============================================================

-   Do not rush the treatment-intensity example. It is the easiest way
    to make the methodology intuitive.
-   Do not read citations aloud on the conceptual-framework slide.
-   Use “associated with” or “related to” for the final results unless
    explicitly describing the identifying hypothesis.
-   Do not describe nighttime lights as GDP. Call them a proxy for local
    economic activity.
-   Do not say the reopening is the “strongest” or “clearest” causal
    evidence. Call it complementary mechanism-consistent evidence.
-   When discussing the insignificant preferred estimate, do not frame
    it as the model failing. Explain what the attenuation tells us about
    regional confounding.
-   If challenged on pre-trends, agree with the econometric implication
    and explain that this is why the final paper limits its causal
    claims.
-   If time is running short, compress the conceptual-framework
    literature discussion and the reopening discussion. Do not cut the
    main-results or dynamic-evidence explanation.
-   Aim to reach the conclusion with at least 2 minutes remaining.
